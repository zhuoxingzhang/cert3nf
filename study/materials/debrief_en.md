# Debrief (read or handed out after the second session)

DRAFT 2026-10-31.

Thank you for taking part. Here is what the study is about.

Database schemas are designed from rules such as "an order belongs to one customer". Software can find many
candidate rules in data, but most of them hold only by accident in the rows at hand. Our method asks a designer a
small number of questions and proves from the answers alone that the schema is correct. Some of the questions you
answered are of a new kind: they list many attributes and ask whether a rule must hold when records agree on all
of them.

The study measures how reliably people answer the different kinds of questions, how long they take, whether their
mistakes repeat when a question is asked again (we asked some questions twice), and whether people make the same
mistakes as a language model. The first questions of each session were the same for all participants, so that their
answers can be compared. Some participants read the documentation of the table and some did not; this is how we
measure what the documentation contributes.

before <date to be set in the ethics application>, contact <researcher, e-mail>.
